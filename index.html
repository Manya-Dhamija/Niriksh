ABOUT NIRIKSH

Niriksh means “close observation.”

From the Sanskrit nirikshan — to inspect, to survey, to look closely before deciding. That's the whole premise: a solar developer shouldn't have to choose a site on a hunch, a broker's word, or a single satellite photo.

WHY WE BUILT THIS

Site selection was six spreadsheets and a gut feeling.

Land teams evaluating solar sites usually pull irradiance data from one source, terrain from another, grid maps from a state utility PDF, and environmental constraints from whoever answers the phone. Every parcel gets judged on whatever data happened to be easiest to find that week.

Niriksh puts land, solar potential, terrain, grid proximity, environmental constraints and climate risk on the same footing, so a 40-acre parcel in one state can be compared honestly against one three states away.

HOW WE SEE A SITE

From raw layers to one ranked list.

  1. 01

    Ingest

    Pull land parcels, solar irradiance, terrain elevation, grid infrastructure, protected-area boundaries and climate hazard layers for the region.

  2. 02

    Normalize

    Convert every layer — wildly different units and scales — into a comparable 0–100 sub-score, so slope and substation distance can sit side by side.

  3. 03

    Weight

    Combine sub-scores using weights that reflect your priorities — grid access matters more in some states, land cost more in others.

  4. 04

    Rank

    Output one Niriksh Score per parcel, and rank the whole shortlist so the best sites rise to the top automatically.
